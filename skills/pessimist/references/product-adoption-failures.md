# Product & adoption failures

The cruelest failure: it works perfectly and nobody cares. A technically flawless thing that solves the wrong problem, or that no one will adopt, is still a total failure. Ask: **"what if we build it exactly as planned — and it doesn't matter?"**

## Wrong problem / no demand
- What's the evidence anyone actually wants this — beyond the builder's conviction? Has a real user ever asked for it?
- Is this solving a real, painful, frequent problem, or a hypothetical one that sounds good in a pitch?
- Are you building it because users need it, or because it's interesting/easy/fun to build?
- Is the problem real but too small — a vitamin, not a painkiller — so no one changes behavior for it?
- What if the few people who want it aren't enough to justify the cost of building/running it?

## Adoption friction
- What does a new user have to do before getting any value? Every step is a place they quit.
- Does it require setup, configuration, migration, integration, or a behavior change? Most people won't.
- Does it assume users will read docs, configure options, or "just get it"? They won't.
- Is there a cold-start problem (no value until there's data/content/other users already present)?
- What's the switching cost from whatever they use now (even if "now" is a spreadsheet or doing nothing)? Inertia usually wins.

## Usability & accessibility
- Where will users get confused, click the wrong thing, or assume it works differently than it does?
- What happens when a user makes a mistake — is it recoverable, or punishing?
- Is it usable on the devices/contexts users actually have (mobile, slow network, small screen, keyboard-only, screen reader)?
- Does it fail for users with disabilities, in other languages, or in other regions — cutting off whole populations?
- Does the empty state, the error state, and the first-run experience make sense, or only the happy demo path?

## Scope & focus
- Is scope creeping — every stakeholder's pet feature bolted on until the thing is bloated and ships late or never?
- Is the MVP actually minimal, or a "minimal" that's secretly six months of work?
- Does adding features make it worse — more confusing, slower, harder to maintain — past the point of usefulness?
- Is the team polishing a corner nobody sees while the core value is still broken?

## Market & competition
- Who already does this, and why would anyone switch from them to you?
- What if a bigger/faster competitor (or the platform itself) ships this as a free feature?
- What if the market window closes — you're too early (no one ready) or too late (everyone served)?
- Is the timing wrong — economic climate, platform shift, attention elsewhere?

## Retention & long game
- Even if users try it once, why would they come back? What makes them stick vs. churn after the novelty?
- Does value decay (a tool used once and abandoned), or compound (gets better the more it's used)?
- What's the failure mode where usage looks fine for a month, then quietly bleeds to zero?
